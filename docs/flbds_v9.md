## 1. Goals

### 1.1 Goal 1: a visual biology-to-engineering thesaurus of existing solutions

Designers lack biological knowledge, and biology and engineering use incompatible vocabularies. The thesaurus is a searchable, evidence-grounded knowledge base of **Mechanism Cards**, each describing how one existing solution (biological or engineered) achieves a function in domain-neutral causal terms. Images are evidence and entry points. Cross-domain links are made only through shared function and physical-effect nodes, never through visual similarity.

- **R1.1** Photo in; ranked organisms, structures, behaviours and linked cards out, with an explicit "unknown" when confidence is low.
- **R1.2** Query by function or problem text, returning biological and engineering entries side by side.
- **R1.3** Navigate abstraction levels up and down from any card.
- **R1.4** Every claim traces to a source span or figure; unevidenced fields are marked, never silently filled.
- **R1.5** Quantities carry units and regime of validity.
- **R1.6** Confidence and provenance on every card; a human-verified tier kept separate.
- **R1.7** First-release coverage: as many silver cards as the corpus yields (target in §6), 100 gold cards, and 300 gold cross-domain pairs.

**Visual means:** entry point (photo to candidate entries), evidence (figure regions linked to the part they show), and presentation (images beside the causal explanation). Visual embeddings are used only inside one domain (to recognise an organism or find similar drawings), never across domains.

**Mechanism Card fields:** id and domain (bio or eng); Subject (taxon path or artefact with CPC class); Context (habitat or operating environment, scale, regime); Function (controlled-vocabulary action plus object); State change; Phenomenon; Effect (canonical physical effect with variables); Input; oRgan and Part; Quantities (value, unit, regime of validity); Constraints and resources; Evidence (text spans, figure regions, DOI or patent number); Abstraction ladder; Quality (confidence, extraction agreement, human-verified flag, licence).

**Abstraction ladder:** L0 specimen; L1 mechanism; L2 causal chain (SAPPhIRE graph); L3 solution-neutral principle; L4 function. Cross-domain retrieval runs at L2 to L4 only.

**Worked ladder (an elephant wallowing in mud).** L0 specimen: an African elephant wallowing in mud. L1 mechanism: a porous mud layer retains water; the retained water evaporates and absorbs latent heat; large body mass buffers daily temperature swings. L2 causal chain: the SAPPhIRE graph of that process. L3 solution-neutral principle: a porous matrix holds a working fluid whose phase change absorbs heat; thermal mass damps the cycle; the matrix is regenerated by re-wetting. L4 function: keep a body below a temperature limit under heat load.

**Non-goals:** a general biology encyclopedia, and inferring physiology from pixels.

### 1.2 Goal 2: novel engineering solutions from a biological image and an engineering image

Input: Image\_Bio (optional caption), Image\_Eng, an optional one-line goal and optional hard constraints. Output: a **Concept Package** of 3 to 5 ranked concepts, each with:

1. **Analogy explanation:** role-binding table (source role to target role), the transformations applied and why, disanalogies and risks, with cited evidence.
2. **Verification report:** executable physics checks with numbers, units and pass, fail or not\_modelled, plus the feasible parameter set.
3. **Annotated 2D schematic** (SVG, optional TikZ).
4. **Concept image:** Image\_Eng edited to show the concept in place, labelled illustrative.
5. **Parametric CAD:** CadQuery script and STEP export, with dimensions from the verifier's nominal parameter vector.
6. **Novelty report:** nearest prior art, abstraction-level distance, and what is genuinely new.

**Functional, not geometric, in testable terms:** a correct output may look nothing like Image\_Bio; no shape-similarity term appears in scoring; every source role is bound, transformed by a named operator, or dropped with a reason; anything infeasible in the target environment is rejected by an executable check. **No pipeline can guarantee a novel invention.** Novelty is measured (prior-art distance and expert ratings), and many outputs will be classed "known solution, re-derived", which counts as validation.

**Non-goals:** full multiphysics certification (FEA or CFD), manufacturing planning, guaranteed patentability.

### 1.3 Failure modes the design targets

| Id | Failure | Elephant-case example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | Morphological leakage (matching by appearance) | Rough mud texture leads to a rough-stone facade |
| F2 | Causal inversion or phase shift missed | The elephant dumps heat by day; the wall must store by day and release at night |
| F3 | Resource disanalogy missed | Evaporation consumes water, which a desert lacks |
| F4 | Scale and regime mismatch | A 1 mm mud layer has negligible capacity at building scale |
| F5 | Unverifiable ideation, or gaming the screen | Fluent text with no numbers; or passing a lumped check with parameters that break a coupled one |

### 1.4 Success criteria (proposed; final values set after Phase 0 baselines)

- **Extraction (100 gold cards, so intervals are wide):** field-level F1 at least 0.80 on Effect, Action and State change; evidence grounding at least 0.90; quantity accuracy at least 0.85.
- **Retrieval:** cross-domain Recall@10 above the strongest baseline (dense retrieval over raw text), with human-judged shape-only leakage at most 10%.
- **Image entry (held-out images):** top-5 organism accuracy at least 0.85; top-5 structure or behaviour at least 0.60; abstention AUROC at least 0.80. The "unknown" rate must be reported.
- **Design validity:** reported per split with confidence intervals. No single overall threshold is claimed, because Split 3 is built to expose the limits of lumped screening.
- **Rediscovery (Split 1):** the documented solution recovered at abstraction level L3 or better in at least 60% of cases, against the prompted baseline.
- **Geometry:** every emitted CadQuery script executes; at least 95% of solids are watertight; dimensions match the verifier's nominal vector.

### 1.5 Contributions and how each is tested

| Id | Contribution | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Image-to-literature causal grounding: photo to organism and behaviour tags, to retrieved papers, to a quantitative card | Held-out image study; extraction F1; ablation A-Img |
| C2 | Functional embedding with morphological-leakage control (three heads, adversarial removal of shape and taxon cues) | Retrieval study: Recall@K and leakage against baselines, plus A-Vis |
| C3 | Constraint-driven causal operators T1 to T5 over SAPPhIRE graphs | Design study: B3 vs A-NoOp (H3a) |

| C6 | Contamination-controlled benchmark of 36 test and 12 dev cases | Release; rater agreement |

**A concept counts as novel only if** (a) its nearest prior art is far at L2 to L3, (b) every source role is bound, transformed or dropped with a reason, and (c) it passes the physics checks. The prior-art index is small (§6), so "new" means "not found in this index" and is reported descriptively with expert labels.

## 2. Thesis and pre-registered hypotheses

**Thesis.** In bio-inspired design for thermal, fluid and porous-media systems, coupling explicit causal graph-rewrite operators (T1 to T5) over SAPPhIRE graphs with a generation-time physics solver (M5-Screen) increases held-out physical validity (M5-Eval) and expert-rated analogical validity over matched-budget prompting, retrieval-augmented prompting and LLM-written verification, on unperturbed and in-operator problems (Splits 1 and 2). A comparison with a coupled-screen oracle on Split 3 separates failures of verifier coverage from failures of analogy transfer. A separate retrieval and image study tests Goal 1.

The unit of analysis is the **mechanism cluster** (12 test clusters, each used in three splits). Seeds are averaged within a case first.

**Design-study hypotheses (Holm-corrected family of five):**

- **H1** B3 beats B1 (matched-budget retrieval plus self-critique) on validity (PVR\_eval).
- **H2** B3 beats B2-Code (the model writes and runs its own verification code) on PVR\_eval.
- **H3a** On Split 2, B3 beats A-NoOp (screen loop without operators) on PVR\_eval.
- **H4** On Split 3, B3-Screen+ beats B3 on PVR\_eval.
- **H5** Blind experts rate B3 above B1 on analogical validity across the 12 clusters (Splits 1 and 2).

**Reported separately, not in the family:**

- **H3b (bio non-inferiority).** B3 is no worse than B3-NoBio (same pipeline, engineering sources only) on PVR\_eval, within a margin of 0.10 on cluster means, and the template distribution is compared descriptively. The value of the biological source is tested by H5, not by H3b.
- **Retrieval and image study (G1 to G3):** extraction F1 on gold cards; Recall@10 and leakage for the functional embedding vs raw-text dense retrieval; held-out image-entry accuracy and abstention. These use bootstrap confidence intervals and make no multiple-comparison claims.

**Naming.** For the untrained pipeline, the drop from screen to evaluator pass rate is the **unmodelled-coupling gap**. "Goodhart drift" is reserved for the trained condition B4.

## 3. Design principles

| Id | Principle | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Vision identifies; literature explains | Images give organism, structure, behaviour, scale and environment. Mechanisms come from retrieved papers. |
| P2 | Bridge only through function and effect | Cross-domain edges exist only between canonical Effect, Action and StateVariable nodes. |
| P3 | LLMs propose; rules rewrite; code verifies | No concept is accepted on fluent text alone. |
| P4 | Prompted first, trained only if it wins | Every trained component has a prompted baseline and a go or no-go gate (§9). |
| P5 | Evaluation before engineering | Dev cases and the evaluator are built first; the test set is sealed. |
| P6 | Evidence or silence | Unsupported fields stay empty and flagged. |
| P7 | Separate the screen from the test | M5-Screen guides generation; M5-Eval scores the result and is never seen by the generator. |
| P8 | Test beyond the rules you wrote | Split 3 uses constraints no operator handles. |
| P9 | Case-level statistics | Seeds measure stability; significance comes from the 12 clusters. |
| P10 | Every case must be satisfiable | A code-generated reference design must pass M5-Eval before a case is admitted. |

**Failure handling:** low recognition confidence returns candidate tags for the designer to confirm; a behaviour with no literature support returns a "hypothesis only" card that cannot back a verified claim; a not\_modelled verdict is never counted as a pass and caps a concept at "screening-level only".

### 4.3 Schemas and controlled vocabularies

- **Mechanism Card:** the fields in §1.1, implemented as Pydantic v2 models (reported implemented, with 15 tests passing; re-run to confirm). SAPPhIRE constructs: State change, Action, Part, Phenomenon, Input, oRgan, Effect.
- **Problem Card (visible to the generator):** problem\_id; target system and length scale; environment (ambient temperature, humidity, resources available and absent as enums, hazards as enums); required function (state variable, target direction, boundary type, flux or load); time pattern; hard constraints (thickness, mass per area, pressure drop, surface superheat, minimum pore diameter, minimum COP, envelope height). **Contains no template names.**
- **ground\_truth\_eval.json (hidden from the generator):** expected conflicts, expected operators, a **list of acceptable templates per case** (operator T4 deliberately changes the template), source card id, reference design file.
- **Enums:** `ResourceEnum` (liquid water, water vapour, ambient air, dry inert gas, solar irradiance, night-sky sink, waste-heat stream, grid electricity, pressurised gas), `HazardEnum` (none, high humidity, dust fouling, icing, low source temperature), `DirectionEnum` (increase, decrease, stabilise, store-and-release, transport-directional), `TimeModeEnum`, `BoundaryTypeEnum` (BC\_FLUX, BC\_TEMP), `DuctCrossSectionEnum` (with Poiseuille numbers), `TemplateEnum` (TPL\_01 to TPL\_10).
- **Typed parameters:** a discriminated union keyed by the chosen template, so each template has its own parameter fields instead of one flat vector.
- **Role binding:** source node, target node, relation (preserved, transformed, substituted, dropped), operator, justification, and the source-card node ids it cites. A binding without valid ids scores zero.
- **Concept Package:** concept id, rank, source cards, problem card, role bindings, transformations, verification report, novelty report, artefacts (explanation, SVG, CadQuery script, STEP file, concept image), disanalogies, risks.

### 4.4 Operators

Operators are typed graph rewrites with a precondition, a rewrite rule and a proof obligation for M5. An LLM proposes which to try; the symbolic engine checks the precondition and applies the rewrite.

| Op | Trigger | Rewrite | Proof obligation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 resource-loop closing | A consumable input is in the absent resources | Sets consumable to false; adds a sealed boundary and a regeneration phase | Net consumable loss is zero over the cycle |
| T2 flow inversion | Source direction opposes target direction | Reverses the flux sign; swaps source and sink roles | Signed energy or mass transfer matches the target |
| T3 temporal phase shift | Steady source, cyclic target | Adds charge and discharge phases around a storage node | Cyclic energy closure |
| T4 effect substitution | Source effect needs a blocked medium or phase | Swaps for a sibling effect with the same state variable | Substituted template conserves |
| T5 regime re-scaling (post-trial) | A dimensionless group (Fo, Bi, Bo, Re) leaves its range using the trial's actual properties | Adds sub-scale structure (matrix, pores, fins) instead of copying lengths | Group inside range on the next trial |
| T6 material substitution | Source medium unavailable | Looks up an engineering material meeting property bounds in the curated table | Property bounds met (DEMONSTRATED) |
| T7 hybridisation | One card covers part of the function | Splices two cards at a shared node | Combined balance closes (OPTIONAL) |

### 4.5 Module details

**M1 Bio grounding (Image\_Bio to Mechanism Cards).**

1. Recognition: a frozen BioCLIP-class model proposes the top-k taxa; a VLM extracts the visible structure, the behaviour (wallowing, basking, burrowing), scale cues and the environment, and abstains when unsure.
2. Query planning: the VLM output becomes literature queries at three levels: taxon plus behaviour; behaviour plus functional goal; structure plus function.
3. Retrieval: hybrid search (keyword, functional embedding, citation-graph expansion) over the corpus, then a cross-encoder rerank.
4. Extraction: the extractor reads the top chunks and writes Mechanism Cards with evidence spans. One organism can yield several cards, one per function (for the elephant: thermoregulation, solar shielding, parasite defence), because the right one depends on the target problem.
5. Conflicts: if sources disagree on a value, the card stores the range and both citations.
6. Output: ranked cards, each with a confidence score and an evidence trail.

**M2 Target understanding (Image\_Eng and goal to Problem Card).**

1. Parse the image: system, components, environment cues and approximate scale. For patent drawings, link reference numerals to component names.
2. Capture the goal and constraints; ask at most one clarifying question.
3. Build a resource inventory: what is abundant and what is absent.
4. Record the time pattern: cycle length and phases.
5. Formulate contradictions as improving versus worsening parameters in the TRIZ style, with BioTRIZ's six fields as a coarse bridge to biological resolutions.
6. Estimate rough size, area and mass as ranges flagged unverified; the designer can override them.

**M3 Thesaurus (storage and retrieval).** Graph nodes: Card, Document, Subject (taxon or artefact), Effect, Action, StateVariable, Phenomenon, Input, Part, oRgan, Evidence (text span or figure region), Image and MechanismCommunity. SAPPhIRE edges: Part creates oRgan; oRgan and Input co-activate Effect; Effect creates Phenomenon; Phenomenon creates State change; State change is interpreted as Action. Card edges: uses Effect, realises Action, has Evidence, about Subject, in Community. Cards from the two domains meet only at Effect, Action and StateVariable nodes. The ontology backbone is a canonical Effect list (physical laws with their variables and dimensions), a controlled Action vocabulary and an organism taxonomy; entity resolution maps extracted phrases onto these nodes, and the 500 most frequent are reviewed by hand. Each card has three vectors: purpose (from L4), mechanism (from L3) and effect (from L2). Retrieval modes: (1) function or problem text to cards from both domains; (2) a card to analogous cards in the other domain; (3) an image to organism, structure and behaviour nodes, then to cards; (4) a Problem Card to ranked mechanisms, diversified across communities.

**M4 Analogy and transformation engine.** Stage A, candidate retrieval: query with the problem's function, time pattern and constraints; return cards at L2 to L4, diversified by community. Stage B, role alignment: each source role is preserved, transformed, substituted or dropped, with a reason. Stage C, disanalogy detection by rules, with typed flags: resource (needs something absent), direction (wrong sign), timing (cycle mismatch), regime (a dimensionless group outside its range) and medium (material unavailable). Stage D, operators (§4.4). Stage E, concept variants: three to five per problem, with components, materials and geometry intent, drawing component ideas from engineering cards. Stage F, ranking by mapping completeness, verified feasibility, novelty and simplicity, with diversity enforced by community.

**M6 Novelty and prior-art gate.** The index holds all cards and patent text embedded at effect and mechanism level. For each concept, retrieve the nearest neighbours at L2 and L3, compute an abstraction-level distance plus a text distance, and have a judge model calibrated on expert labels compare the concept with its ten closest items and classify it: known solution (re-derived); known in engineering with a new biological transfer path; new combination of known parts; or new transfer with no close prior art. The report lists the closest known solutions and what differs.

**M7 Output generation.** Explanation: generated from the Concept Package, citing only linked evidence. SVG schematic: a code model turns a structured layout specification (layers, materials, flows, dimensions) into SVG, and a VLM critic inspects the render and requests repairs. CadQuery: macro geometry is built from the verifier's nominal parameter vector and exported to STEP and STL; porous or cellular parts use a lattice helper or the Bioinspired3D route; every artefact is checked for execution, watertightness, bounding box and parameter match. Concept image: an image-editing model conditioned on Image\_Eng and the schematic, always labelled illustrative.

**M8 Designer interface and logging.** The designer can change the function, abstraction level, constraints or operator set; pin or exclude a mechanism; compare concepts side by side; and see the evidence behind every claim. The role-binding table, disanalogies and verification report are always visible. Every run logs its inputs, retrieved cards, operator choices, verifier outputs and designer actions, so ablations can be replayed. A fixation guard presents results in diverse groups and encourages exploring alternatives before committing.

## 5. Physics verifier (M5): screen vs evaluator

**Principles.** A human-written, unit-tested Python library (`flbds_physics`) using Pint, SymPy and SciPy. The LLM selects a template and fills a typed parameter object; it never writes equations. Scripts run in a sandbox with no network, a timeout and fixed seeds. Verdicts are pass, fail or not\_modelled. Both tiers enforce every hard constraint on the Problem Card. M5-Eval is hidden from the generator except in the oracle condition B3-Screen+.

**Boundary conditions.** Every check declares one. BC\_FLUX applies a gross absorbed flux q\_abs (W/m²) at the outer face; outdoor convective and radiative exchange is a separate Robin condition (h\_out, ambient and sky temperatures) in the evaluator's cycle solver. The two are never mixed inside one closed-form check. BC\_TEMP applies a fixed surface excess.

| ID | Template | M5-Screen (lumped, plus explicit constraints) | M5-Eval (coupled, held out) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TPL-01 | Transient conduction and thermal mass | Stored energy at least required; Fo in \[0.25, 5\]; thickness and mass caps | 24 h cyclic solve with Robin boundary: swing attenuation and phase lag |
| TPL-02 | Solid-liquid PCM storage | Enthalpy closure with composite properties; effective Fo at least 0.8; caps | 1D enthalpy-method solver: melt depth within the layer, end-of-charge surface superheat within its limit, stored energy at least 0.9 of required after outdoor loss. Cross-checked against the Neumann solution (t\_melt = L²/(4 α λ²)) for BC\_TEMP |
| TPL-03 | Evaporation and condensation | Latent flux at least required; water budget | Evaporation: wet-bulb and Chilton–Colburn mass transfer at ambient humidity. Condensation: the air stream's humidity-ratio capacity must cover the required condensate, plus a surface-temperature check |
| TPL-04 | Closed-loop sorption storage | Storage density from bulk density (no extra porosity factor) at least required; zero water loss | Regeneration temperature reachable from the source; linear-driving-force time at most 0.15 of the charge period; uptake at operating conditions |
| TPL-05 | Capillary wicking | Jurin height at least 1.5 × lift; Bond number below 0.1 | Washburn and Darcy supply at the lift height meets required mass flow; viscous drop within cap; pore clearance above the fouling limit |
| TPL-06 | Darcy–Forchheimer flow | Darcy drop; pore Reynolds number at most 10 | Ergun drop (viscous plus inertial) within cap; pumping COP at least the minimum; thermal duty by NTU–effectiveness for regenerator beds |
| TPL-07 | Fin convection | Fin efficiency; Bi below 0.1; envelope height | Boundary-layer choking (spacing at least 2δ) or Elenbaas channel correlation; degraded duty at least required |
| TPL-08 | Countercurrent exchanger | ε–NTU duty; length cap | Axial-conduction degradation; Poiseuille drop using the duct cross-section number; drop within cap |
| TPL-09 | Radiative–convective exchange | Net surface flux balance; thickness and mass caps | Quartic equilibrium with conduction and cavity radiation; inner-face leakage within cap |
| TPL-10 | Fickian and Knudsen diffusion | Flux at least required; mass Fo at least 1 | Bosanquet transition plus external mass-transfer resistance |

**Outside the ten templates** (heat-pipe and thermosyphon loops, impaction-based capture): a case needing them is rewritten or dropped. Concepts in these areas return not\_modelled.

### 5.1 Satisfiability gate (principle P10)

Before any case is admitted: (1) a **capacity bound** is computed: maximum storable energy per area is thickness × the best admissible volumetric capacity inside the temperature window. Checked values: paraffin-class (820 kg/m³, 210 kJ/kg) gives 1.72 MJ/m² at 10 mm, so a 5.76 MJ/m² load in 10 mm is impossible for paraffin; a hydrate-class material (1560 kg/m³, 191 kJ/kg) gives at most 6.56 MJ/m² at 22 mm with no matrix; (2) a **reference design file generated by the solver** must pass M5-Eval; (3) all acceptable templates are listed. An automated pytest run covers all 48 cases (12 dev, 36 test). A failing case is rewritten or dropped. No design is admitted on hand-typed numbers; every reference design is solver-generated.

### 5.2 Template validation

Each template is tested on at least three textbook worked problems (for example Incropera and DeWitt, and Bear; confirm access) within 1% tolerance; TPL-02 is cross-checked against the Neumann solution and against a finer mesh; mutation tests perturb a passing design to confirm it fails; unit-mismatch tests confirm Pint rejects bad inputs.

### 7.2 Metrics

- **PVR\_eval** (headline): 1 if the chosen template is in the case's acceptable list and the typed parameters pass M5-Eval, else 0. PVR\_screen is the same with M5-Screen.
- **Unmodelled-coupling gap:** PVR\_screen minus PVR\_eval, per condition and split.
- **Expert analogical validity** (1 to 5, blind, §8): the headline measure of whether the biology was used.
- **CMF and BGVP (secondary, descriptive):** CMF checks that source roles are bound with valid card ids, that expected conflicts are flagged with the right operator, and that the biological organ appears as a non-degenerate parameter. They are calibrated against the expert ratings and not used as a headline, because the model reports its own bindings and a standard PCM-in-foam design would satisfy the structural rule.
- **Rediscovery level** (Split 1): L0 to L4; success is L3 or better.
- **Concept Package completeness:** share of runs in which all six outputs are present, SVG renders, CadQuery executes, and solids are watertight with dimensions matching the nominal vector.
- **Novelty class:** known solution re-derived, known in engineering with a new transfer path, new combination, or new transfer, reported descriptively.

### 7.3 Statistical plan

1. Average the five seeds within each case first. The 12 mechanism clusters are the independent units.
2. **Primary tests:** paired exact permutation test (4,096 sign flips) on cluster means, with Pratt-adjusted Wilcoxon as a check; report the number of clusters with non-zero differences.
3. **Co-primary model:** a binomial mixed model with condition and split fixed and random intercepts for mechanism and for case within mechanism, with cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples of the 12 clusters). If ties leave fewer than 9 informative clusters, the mixed model is primary.
4. Holm correction across H1, H2, H3a, H4 and H5. H3b is reported as a non-inferiority interval with margin 0.10.
5. Effect sizes with intervals; mean within-case seed spread; a threshold-sensitivity curve relaxing and tightening M5-Eval bounds by 10, 20 and 30 percent.
6. Everything is pre-registered in the repository before the freeze tag.

### 9.3 Training stack and starting recipes

**Stack.** PyTorch with FlashAttention; TRL or Unsloth for supervised fine-tuning and DPO; 8-bit paged AdamW; BF16; gradient checkpointing; sequence packing. vLLM for all batch generation (FP8 for models of 30B and larger) with prefix caching. Pint, SymPy and SciPy for physics.

**Memory.** A 14B model in BF16 needs about 28 GB of weights and an 8B model about 16 GB, so both train comfortably with checkpointing on one H200. Full-parameter tuning of 8B models is not planned, because optimizer state would approach the 141 GB limit.

**Starting values (tuned in Phase 0).**

- LoRA supervised fine-tuning: rank 64, alpha 128, dropout 0.05, learning rate 1e-4 with cosine decay, 2 epochs, packed sequences up to 8k tokens.
- Offline DPO: beta 0.1, learning rate 5e-6 with LoRA, 1 to 2 epochs, reference log-probabilities computed once.
- Contrastive embedding: InfoNCE with temperature 0.05, batch of 1,024 with hard negatives, learning rate 2e-5, 3 epochs.
- Adversarial leakage head: gradient reversal with its weight ramped from 0 to 0.3.
- Preference data is regenerated from the new policy for at most 2 rounds.

Every job logs tokens per second and saves resumable checkpoints, so drift from the planning figures shows within hours. CPU sandbox work overlaps GPU generation so the GPU is not left idle.

## 12. Worked trace and verified numbers: elephant mud to desert heat-storage wall (probe, `DEV-M1` Split 2)

**Source card (bio).** Elephant skin and mud: action regulate body temperature; effect latent heat of vaporisation (about 2.4 MJ/kg) with capillary retention and sensible thermal mass; input liquid water (consumable); organ a crevice network holding water and mud. Field values from the physiology literature must be verified before use.

**Problem Card.** Desert shelter wall. Absent: liquid water. Available: solar radiation, ambient air, clear night sky. Function: store heat by day (q\_abs = 200 W/m² for 8 h, 5.76 MJ/m²) and release indoors at night. Constraints: no consumable water; mass at most 45 kg/m²; thickness at most 50 mm; end-of-charge surface superheat at most 10 K.

**Conflicts and operators.** Resource (water absent): T1 seals the loop. Direction (the elephant rejects heat; the wall must absorb): T2. Timing (continuous evaporation vs a charge and discharge cycle): T3. Effect (open evaporation not allowed): T4 substitutes solid-liquid phase change. Regime (pure paraffin has too little conductivity): T5 adds a conductive matrix.

**Charging check (illustrative property values, to be replaced by cited data; numerical 1D enthalpy solver, constant flux, adiabatic back face, starting at the melting point).**

| Design | Mass | Result at the end of 8 h | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pure paraffin, 50 mm (k = 0.2 W/m·K) | 41 kg/m² | Surface superheat **26.5 K**; melt depth 29.5 mm. The closed form predicts 35.3 K, an overestimate | Fails the 10 K limit |
| 88% paraffin and 12% aluminium foam, 40 mm (assumed k\_eff = 2.8 W/m·K) | **41.8 kg/m²** | Latent capacity 6.06 MJ/m² against 5.76 required; melt depth 37.6 mm; surface superheat **2.62 K** (closed form 2.64 K); effective Fo about 28 | Passes |

**What this does not show.** It covers the charging half only. The evaluator must also solve the 24 h cycle with outdoor loss and the night discharge, and that solver is not yet validated. The 2.8 W/m·K effective conductivity needs a foam correlation or a cited value. The expected novelty verdict is "known in the engineering domain", because phase-change walls in foam matrices are documented, so the probe tests causal inversion and is not a claim of invention.

## Appendix A. Abstract

FLBDS identifies the organism and behaviour in a photograph, retrieves the literature that explains the physics, and stores the result as a quantitative SAPPhIRE Mechanism Card in a function-indexed thesaurus that links biological and engineering solutions only through shared physical effects. Given a second photograph of an engineering target, it binds roles between the two, detects where the biological mechanism conflicts with the target (absent resources, inverted direction, shifted timing, mismatched scale), applies explicit graph-rewrite operators, checks each concept with executable lumped-parameter physics, classifies its novelty against prior art, and emits an analogy explanation, verification report, SVG schematic, CadQuery model and illustrative concept image. For thermal, fluid and porous-media problems, we evaluate the pipeline against matched-budget prompting, retrieval, and LLM-written verification on 36 held-out cases built from 12 mechanism clusters, scored by a held-out coupled-physics evaluator and by blind expert audits that measure both false assurance and false rejection, with a separate retrieval and image-entry study for the thesaurus.
